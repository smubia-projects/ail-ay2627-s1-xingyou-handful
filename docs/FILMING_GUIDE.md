# Handful filming guide

> **Not needed right now.** Handful's sign videos come from [ASL Signbank](https://aslsignbank.com) (see
> `docs/CREDITS.md`). This guide is kept for the day Handful films its own videos. To match the Signbank
> clips, film against a plain **teal** background.

Everything needed to film the sign videos for Handful and hand them over. Read sections 1–5 once,
then use the lists in section 7 while filming.

## 1. Who should sign

**A Deaf or fluent ASL signer should be the person on camera.** Learners copy exactly what they
see, so a video signed by someone still learning teaches mistakes that the learner cannot spot.

If no fluent signer is available yet, clips filmed by a non-signer can be used as **placeholders
only**, and each one must be approved by a Deaf ASL signer before the app is released.

Things to settle before filming:
- **The signer's face will be visible in the app.** Facial expression is part of ASL (eyebrows,
  headshakes, emotion), so the face cannot be cropped out or hidden.
- Once the app is online, anyone who uses it can see these videos. Get the signer's written
  agreement to that first.
- Use **one signer for a whole lesson**. Up to four signers in total across the app is fine.

## 2. Equipment and setup

- **Camera:** a phone is fine. Use the **back camera**, turned **sideways (landscape)**.
- **Phone settings (iPhone):** Settings → Camera → Record Video → **1080p HD at 30 fps**.
  Settings → Camera → Formats → **Most Compatible**.
- **Tripod or a stable surface.** Never handheld. Do not move or zoom the camera between clips.
- **Camera height:** level with the signer's chest, pointing straight at them.
- **Background:** a plain, light wall with nothing on it (white, cream, light grey or beige).
- **Light:** face a window or lamp so the light falls on the signer's front. No window or lamp
  behind them, and no strong shadows on the face or hands.
- **Clothing:** a plain top in one solid color that contrasts with the signer's skin and with
  the wall. No patterns, logos or text. Short or fitted sleeves.
- **Hands and face:** no rings, watch, bracelets or long nails. Hair tied back from the face.
- **Sound:** the app plays videos silently, but the file still records sound. Film in a quiet
  room and do not talk during a clip.

## 3. Framing

Mark the floor with tape so the signer stands in the same spot for every clip.

- The picture shows from **just above the head down to the waist**.
- The signer is **centered**, facing the camera, standing or sitting upright.
- Leave an arm's width of empty space on each side. With both arms stretched out sideways, the
  **hands must still be inside the picture**.
- Check once at the start, then do not change the distance, zoom or angle.

## 4. Which hand

- Right-handed signers sign with the **right hand as the main hand**.
- **Left-handed signers sign naturally with their left hand.** Do not force the other hand: it
  looks stiff. Say so when handing the clips over, so those clips can be marked as left-handed.
- Two-handed signs use both hands as described.
- Try to keep to one handedness within a lesson, so learners are not switching back and forth.

## 5. How to film each clip

One clip per sign. Each clip is **about 4–6 seconds** and follows the same pattern:

1. Start recording. Hands rest at the waist, looking at the camera. Hold for **1 second**.
2. Make the sign **once**, at a clear, natural speed.
3. Hands back to rest. Pause for **half a second**.
4. Make the sign **a second time**, exactly the same.
5. Hands back to rest. Hold for **1 second**. Stop recording.

Rules:
- Every handshape fully formed. No half-closed fingers and no rushed, cut-short movements.
- Use the facial expression that belongs to the sign (see the **Face** notes in the lists).
- Look at the camera, not at your hands.
- If anything goes wrong, **film the whole clip again**. Extra seconds at the start or end are
  fine; they get trimmed.
- Rough clips are fine in format: they will be resized to 1920 × 1080, trimmed and converted.

## 6. Naming and handing over the clips

**Name each file after the sign**, using the exact file name from the lists in section 7.
The ending can be `.mov` or `.mp4`, whichever the phone produces.

To rename a file on a Mac: click it once, press **Return**, type the new name, press **Return**.

**Do a trial run first.** Film only the five Greetings signs (`hello.mov`, `goodbye.mov`, `please.mov`, `thank-you.mov`, `sorry.mov`), hand those over, and wait
for feedback on framing, light and speed before filming the rest. This avoids re-filming
everything.

To hand the clips over:
1. Move the clips from the phone to the Mac (AirDrop, or plug the phone in and use Photos or
   Image Capture).
2. Open the project in VS Code. In the file list on the left, find the **`video-inbox`** folder.
3. **Drag the clips into `video-inbox`.**
4. Tell Claude: "videos are in the inbox". Mention the signer's name or ID, and whether they
   signed left-handed.

What happens next: each clip is resized, trimmed and converted, saved to `public/videos/`, and
linked to its sign. The raw clips in `video-inbox` are never published and are not saved in git.

## 8. Before the app is released

A Deaf ASL signer must watch and approve every video, as described in
`docs/VIDEO_GUIDELINES.md`. If a sign is wrong or unclear, it is filmed again, not edited.
